Rooted in Tradition

Why We Chose Slow Cloth in a World of Fast Fashion

JP Cotton was born from a singular conviction: India's handloom weaving and hand-block stamping is one of humanity's greatest living crafts, yet it was being commoditized by cheap synthetic polyester and high-speed factory printing.

We traveled directly into the historic artisan pockets of Saurashtra, Punjab, Bagru, and Sanganer, partnering directly with master block printers and pit-loom weavers whose families have preserved these techniques for over four generations. We eliminated brokers and middlemen, ensuring fair-trade wages and safe, dignified working studios.

Every piece we create is completely hypoallergenic, free from microplastics, and naturally softened with herbal botanical washes. It is cloth made with reverence for the earth, designed to grow softer and more graceful with every wash.

Atelier Craft Spotlight

The Living Art of Hand-Block Printing

No two impressions are identical. Each piece is stamped hundreds of times by master artisans using seasoned teakwood blocks and skin-safe plant pigments.

The Printing Studio

JP Cotton Handicrafts Studio

Inside our dedicated hand-block printing studio, 10-meter lengths of pure mulmul and cambric cotton are stretched taut across padded tables. Master printers work side by side, dipping hand-carved wooden blocks into mineral and botanical pigment trays before aligning every mandala by eye and breath.

Hand-Chiseled Teakwood

Blocks are carved from seasoned Sheesham and Teak wood using micro-chisels over 7 to 10 days, allowing intricate mandala and floral lace motifs to transfer cleanly to cotton.

Rhythmic Hand Alignment

Without laser guides or machine jigs, the artisan relies on muscle memory and visual registration marks. The subtle variation in ink pressure gives handloom cloth its unmistakable organic soul.

Artisanal Textile Library

Four Signature Handloom Weaves

Each textile in our collections is spun from specific long-staple Indian cotton yarns, developed to celebrate the seasons.

Featherlight Mulmul

The legendary ‘woven wind’ of Bengal. Spun with gossamer-fine 100s count yarn for airy, cloud-soft breathability in summer warmth.

Crisp Cambric & Poplin

Densely set warp and weft spun from long-staple cotton, delivering an immaculate, structured drape for shirts and tailored kurtas.

Chanderi with Zari

A royal celebration fabric interwoven with gold-dipped threads and sheer cotton warp, historically favored by royal courts.

Heavy Handloom Khadi

Rustic, uncombed yarn woven with a rich tactile grain that softens gracefully with every wash, gaining a beloved patina over years.

Farm to Wardrobe

The 5 Artisanal Stages of Creation

Follow the deliberate, mindful journey of our cotton from sun-drenched organic fields to final tailoring.

Stage 01

The Harvest

Hand-harvesting ripe organic cotton bolls under the Saurashtra sun to preserve the staple fiber strength.

Stage 02

Slow Ginning

Gentle mechanical separation of seeds from pure, soft cotton fibers without high-heat chemical stripping.

Stage 03

Ring-Spinning

Twisting the fibers on traditional spinning frames into durable, uniform yarn imbued with natural breathability.

Stage 04

Wooden Looms

Flying shuttles driven rhythmically by master weavers to produce signature open-pore textiles that let skin breathe.

Stage 05

Hand-Block Stamping

Hand-carved wooden blocks stamped rhythmically with natural botanical dyes, followed by bespoke tailoring.

The Makers

Faces Behind The Craft

Meet the generational craftspeople whose hands and hearts shape every garment we create.

Master Block Printer • JP Cotton Workshop

Shri Mohan Lal & The Print Guild

“Every block we press is a prayer for endurance. When machine prints fade or crack, our hand-block natural colors soften like a cherished memory.”

With 35 years at the print table, Mohan Lal leads our guild of 24 block printers, keeping the centuries-old Bagru and Sanganer printing traditions alive with steady hands and pride.

4th Generation Master Weaver • Punjab Guild

Ustad Ram Swaroop & Sons

“The loom is our temple. When you listen closely, the wooden shuttle beats with the heartbeat of our ancestors. Wearing handloom is wearing a living blessing.”

Operating a guild of 35 traditional pit-looms in rural Punjab, Ram Swaroop has preserved endangered twill and mulmul weaving techniques for over four decades, training younger apprentices.

Master Needlecraft Collective • Lucknow & Barmer

Zeenat Begum & The Women's Guild

“For us, embroidery is financial freedom. With JP Cotton's direct remuneration, 120 women in our village earn fair, independent livelihoods while keeping our heritage alive.”

Specializing in delicate Chikankari, Kantha, and Aari threadwork, Zeenat leads a women-owned cooperative ensuring that traditional needle artistry provides dignified economic empowerment.
